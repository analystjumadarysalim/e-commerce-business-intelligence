# Executive Summary: E-Commerce Business Intelligence

Prepared by Salim Abdhallah Jumadary.

Scope: 1,067,371 transactions from a UK online gift-ware wholesaler, December 2009 to December 2011. After cleaning, 39,393 orders and 5,851 identified customers. All figures use adjusted data (order-entry reversals removed).

## What the data says

1. **Growth is flat.** Jan-Nov 2011 revenue was £8.70M, 1.8% above 2010. January to August fell 0.5%; all of the growth came in September to November (+5.4%), driven by larger orders and not more orders.
2. **The business is seasonal.** October to December months bring 35.1% of revenue. November reaches about £1.4M against £0.5M to 0.8M in January to August.
3. **A small group of customers carries the revenue.** 25% of customers bring 70.1% of identified revenue. The top 10% of customers bring 63.2%.
4. **Returns are low once mistakes are separated out.** Genuine refunds are 1.9% of sales (£365K). Almost half of the raw refund value (49.0%) was order-entry reversals made within 24 hours, including one 80,995-unit order worth £168K.
5. **Customers do come back.** 72.3% ordered more than once, and about one in five of a cohort buys in any later month.

## What I would do

| Action | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Plan stock and staffing for the autumn peak | October to December bring 35.1% of revenue; all 2011 growth came in September to November |
| Give the core customers account management | Champions are 25% of customers and 70.1% of revenue |
| Add checks on very large order lines | 1,331 reversed lines (£389K of sales), including a £168K single entry cancelled in 12 minutes |
| Capture customer IDs on large orders | About 13% of revenue (£2.6M) has no customer ID, including 4 of the 5 largest orders |
| Find out why some products are returned so often before reordering | White and Pink Cherry Lights were returned in 123 and 85 cancellations, concentrated in spring 2010 |
| Treat flat growth as the main issue and check new-customer acquisition | Growth was 1.8%; first-time customers look lower in 2011, but the comparison is biased by data history, so verify first |

## Outlook

Base case for December 2011 to March 2012 is £2.60M (range £2.54M to £2.70M), roughly flat on the year before. A backtest on September to November 2011 was 5.6% too low, so treat the forecast as a rough guide. Early December 2011 was running 16% ahead of 2010, but that rests on about seven trading days.

## Caveats

Customers without an ID cannot be tracked or matched to refunds. Two years of data cannot separate trend from seasonality. The reversal window (24 hours) and segment rules are judgement calls. The data cannot explain why customers returned certain products or why September 2011 jumped.
